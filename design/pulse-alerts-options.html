Why it works

The strip compresses 30 alerts into the three numbers the owner actually needs over coffee — 2 urgent (with the rand total), 3 watching, 25 routine — so severity is encoded in hierarchy and hue instead of repetition. Red ink is spent only on the money items; follow-ups read as a neutral work queue with a tertiary dot. Because the detail cards below already carry the underlying data, the expand panels stay shallow and every path deep-links to Reconciliation, Projects, or Clients rather than duplicating rows.

Trade-offs

Individual alert lines are one click away rather than scannable at a glance, so a genuinely new overdue invoice is a number changing from 2 to 3, not a new visible row. The accordion shows only one category's detail at a time, and the 25-name pill cloud truncates at 8 names + "+17 more".

Why it works

It reframes 30 alerts as a three-line briefing ranked by severity, so the owner's first glance lands on the only two items that cost money — already expanded — while 28 routine items collapse into counts with one-click deep links to the detail cards that already exist below. Red ink is spent exclusively on the two overdue invoices (dot, total, amounts); retainers read amber-with-mini-burn-bars and follow-ups read as quiet cyan housekeeping, so the page stops crying wolf. Why it works

A proportional segmented meter compresses 30 alert rows into a single ~90px strip topped by one human sentence, so severity is read at a glance instead of row-by-row. The red ink budget is enforced structurally: red exists only in the 2-item invoice segment and its R60,950 figure, while the 25 follow-ups become a wide calm cyan band that visually says "routine bulk, not fire." Because the detail cards below already hold the same data, each group panel stays shallow — two invoice rows, three mini burn bars, a chip cloud — and deep-links to Reconciliation, Projects and Clients rather than duplicating lists.

Trade-offs

Proportional encoding makes the least-important category (25 routine items) the most visually dominant segment, which a skim-reader could misread as the big story. Specific invoice names need one click (or the headline) — nothing actionable is visible row-level by default.

Why it works

The alert strip was a derived duplicate of the cards below it, so removing it eliminates the redundancy and the daily false alarm in one move. Severity is still legible in under a second: a whisper-weight header pill gives the 2/3/25 triage counts (clickable, deep-linking to the owning card), and each section card wears its own count badge right next to the data you would act on. Red ink is spent only on the two genuine money items — the AR badge, the 30+ tile numeral, and the overdue day counts — so 25 routine follow-ups read as a quiet to-do, not a fire.

Trade-offs

There is no single consolidated list to triage from, so an owner who wants "all 30 in one place" must visit each card — and badges on cards below the fold can be missed. Watch-level retainer items only surface in the header pill (tooltip) here, since the Retainer Burn card isn't part of this two-card mock.

Why it works

It converts the 30-row alert wall into the four numbers an owner actually wants over coffee: cash at risk, retainers running hot, relationship upkeep, and work in flight. Severity is carried by one colored sub-stat line per tile — red exists only on the two overdue invoices, amber on three retainers, and the 25 follow-ups collapse into a single neutral "25 due" count. Trade-offs
