Why it's calmer

The Group column — the single biggest source of chaos — is deleted; each taxonomy name appears exactly once, in a slim sticky section header that doubles as a collapse control and a running count/hours summary. Rows lock to a uniform 40 px. Zeros become faint dots so the few cells that carry hours are the only things your eye lands on, and the rule column is demoted to a dim single line (extra-dim when it merely echoes the group). Inputs are invisible until you hover, so the resting state reads as a document, not a form.

Trade-offs

Eight section headers spend vertical space, and scanning or sorting a single column (e.g. price) across all groups is harder than in one flat list. The grouping is derived from hours, so a service can hop sections after an edit, which may surprise. Two stacked sticky layers (column header + section header) need a tall enough viewport to stay useful, and percentage-priced rows can't show a meaningful %-of-price readout.

OPTION 2

Narrow ledger with an allocation popover

All eight department columns collapse into one fixed-width Allocation cell — total hours, a tiny stacked bar, a dominant-department label — and clicking it opens an anchored popover where the real editing happens.

Why it's calmer

Eight hour columns and a Total collapse into one fixed-width cell, so the wall of grey zeros disappears entirely — zero-hour rows show a single quiet “Set hours” ghost. No horizontal scroll: price sits beside the name, every row is exactly 46px, and group taxonomy lives in a one-line ellipsized chip. Editing moves into a focused anchored popover with steppers, live cost-per-department, and a running % of price, so the grid itself never has to host 8 inputs per row.

Trade-offs

Exact per-department hours are one click away rather than scannable in the grid — the bar conveys shape, not values (segment tooltips and the legend mitigate). Bulk editing across many services means opening popovers one at a time. The 8-colour ramp asks a small learning cost, hence the persistent legend.

Why it's calmer

Every service collapses to a single 44px line, so 139 rows scan like a list, not a spreadsheet. The two noisy taxonomy strings are demoted — rule to a muted ellipsised line, group to a slim single-line chip — so nothing ever wraps. The 8 hour columns (70% zeros) leave the scan path entirely and appear only inside the editor you asked for, with inherited values italic and your touches highlighted amber until saved.

Trade-offs

Hours are not comparable across services at a glance — you must expand a row to see the department split. One-row-at-a-time expansion makes bulk hour edits slower than a true grid, and opening a row reflows the rows beneath it. The status dot is more compact but less explicit than a labelled badge.

Why it's calmer

The 139×12 grid collapses to a two-line list where long taxonomy names appear exactly once — as group captions — and never as wrapping pills. The eight hour columns leave the table entirely: zeros become a dimmed dash in a purpose-built inspector that shows only one service at a time, with full department names, live cost math, share bars, and Save/Cancel that appear only when something actually changed. Row heights are perfectly uniform and price sits right under the name.

Trade-offs

Only one service is inspectable at a time, so cross-service hour comparison needs the detail page or a future compare view. Rule names move off the list (one click away in the inspector), and the split needs roughly 900px of width — small screens would have to stack list and inspector as a push navigation.

OPTION 5

The disciplined spreadsheet

Exactly the table people already trust — same fourteen columns — with every source of noise fixed: one-line chips, ditto-marked rules, dotted zeros, uniform 36px rows, sticky identity columns and hideable departments.

Why it's calmer

Nothing moves for people who live in this grid — same columns, same order — but every noise source is silenced: chips never wrap so every row is exactly 36px; the 3px keel color groups rows pre-attentively without reading a single chip; the ditto mark erases the Rule column's duplicate taxonomy; zeros collapse into faint dots so real hours pop; rates live in the header instead of repeating per row; Total and Save/Cancel stay pinned on the right so editing feedback never scrolls away; and departments you never staff can be switched off entirely from the Columns menu.

Trade-offs

It is still a wide horizontally-scrolling grid — the sticky Group/Code/Name rail and edge shadow mitigate but don't remove that. The ditto mark needs a one-time learning moment (title tooltip helps), ellipsized chips hide the full taxonomy until hover, and 13px/36px density is keyboard-and-mouse territory — touch users would want Option-style row editors. Column visibility is per-session unless persisted.
